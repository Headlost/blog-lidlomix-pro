01 / Beginnings · 13 November 2026

Recipes from a family notebook

When my grandmother said ‘take a handful’, she knew exactly how much that meant. It always turned out delicious. Recipes like hers were passed down through generations, and years of cooking left their marks on the pages. I had one of those notebooks, with stained pages and handwritten notes. Looking through it, I thought how useful it would be to photograph a page and cook from it on my Lidlomix.

A photo of the page

At the time, the models were inconsistent at reading Polish handwriting. A familiar flourish might be obvious to me, yet ChatGPT could read it as an entirely different number. I gave it examples, corrected mistakes and checked whether it could transcribe the whole page and point out anything it was unsure of.

Those experiments led to the ‘Grandma’s recipes’ starter in Lidlomix GPT. You could open it, upload a photo and write ‘Adapt this recipe’. The assistant transcribed the photo and showed what it had found. You could then correct an ingredient, a measurement or any steps it had read out of order. Only after you confirmed the transcription did it prepare a version for Monsieur Cuisine.

How much does a handful weigh?

‘A small pot of cream’, ‘a handful of nuts’ and ‘as much flour as the dough will take’ were instructions for someone who already knew how the recipe worked. To make it on the robot, I needed quantities I could weigh and steps I could follow. The assistant converted the measurements for each ingredient, put the steps in order and selected the programmes, accessories, times, temperatures and speeds.

I could save the adapted recipe as a Word document and take it into the kitchen. But I kept having to set the time, speed and temperature by hand in Manual settings. I had a clear, adapted recipe. What I still wanted was to open it on the robot and follow each step with its settings already in place.

Recipe on the robot screen

I could not find a way to send the adapted recipe from the assistant to the factory app. I also wanted to connect a local tool through MCP, a protocol that lets the assistant submit a recipe to the app for approval. So I started writing Lidlomix Pro from scratch in Rust. The factory screens helped me arrange the controls in a way that would feel familiar. I wrote the catalogue, search and step-by-step guidance myself.

Memory mattered on the Connect too. The app stores recipes in a local SQLite database and loads the results each screen needs instead of putting the entire catalogue in RAM. You can save a recipe from the chat as JSON and send it from your phone over Wi-Fi. It opens under Recipe drafts on the robot, where you can review and approve it. The saved recipe stays on the device. The same file can carry a photo of the dish up to 500 kB.

Today I can go back to the notebook, take a photo and turn a handwritten ‘handful of nuts’ into a recipe on the Connect screen. Each step tells me what to add and which programme to use. The settings are ready, and I press Start when everything is in the bowl.
