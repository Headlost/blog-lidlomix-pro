Privacy policy

The email address you provide in a form is used only to contact you about your request. Data from the blog and forum is used for neither marketing nor training AI models.

Who is responsible for your data

The data controller is Beniamin Żak, a private individual and the creator of Lidlomix Pro. For questions about your data, use the private form on this page.

What data is processed and why

  • Update sign-ups and offers of devices for testing. The controller records your email address, device model and submission date. Your postcode and town are recorded if you provide them. Location helps determine where an update can be carried out or a test device collected. If you sign up for updates, you can add a comment. If you offer a device, the controller records its description. Your email address is used only to reply and follow up on your request, including updates for your chosen model if you ask for them. Processing is based on consent under Article 6(1)(a) of the GDPR. Your email address is kept private.
  • Privacy enquiries. The controller processes your email address, message and submission date to reply to your question or handle your request. The legal basis is compliance with a legal obligation when handling GDPR rights under Article 6(1)(c), and the controller’s legitimate interest in handling correspondence for other enquiries under Article 6(1)(f).
  • Forum and comments. The controller records your name or nickname, post, date and the article or topic it relates to. Bug reports can also include an optional screenshot. Article comments become public on submission. Forum posts become public on submission or after approval, depending on the moderation settings. The legal basis is the controller’s legitimate interest in running and moderating discussions under Article 6(1)(f). Before sending a screenshot, remove any personal data visible on it. External illustrations in the Technical details section load from GitHub.
  • Security and spam prevention. GitHub Pages logs visitors’ IP addresses. The forms server uses a hash of the IP address to limit request frequency. The legal basis for this measure is the controller’s legitimate interest in protecting the service and its users under Article 6(1)(f).

Providing data is voluntary. An email address is needed to reply, and a name and message are required to publish a comment. You can use a nickname. The controller does not profile users or make solely automated decisions that produce legal effects or affect users in a similarly significant way.

Who can access your data

GitHub Pages hosts the blog, OpenAI Sites runs the backend for the forms and forum, and Web3Forms delivers private requests to the controller’s Outlook inbox (Microsoft). Moderators can access posts awaiting approval. Private requests are accessible to the project controller and the service providers needed to handle them. Technical data and requests may be processed outside the European Economic Area in accordance with the relevant providers’ policies. You can ask through the contact form about the basis for a transfer and the safeguards available.

How long data is kept

The controller keeps requests and correspondence for as long as needed to handle them. For update sign-ups, this is the waiting period for an update for the relevant model or until consent is withdrawn. Pending posts are kept until reviewed. Published posts are kept while they form part of the discussion or until they are removed earlier. Data needed to handle a request, defend claims or comply with a legal obligation may be kept for as long as required for that purpose. Service providers set the retention periods for their own technical logs.

Cookies and external links

The blog uses no first-party cookies for advertising or analytics. Links to services such as BuyCoffee.to, Ko-fi, GitHub and ChatGPT open sites with their own privacy policies. An embedded YouTube video, where available in a post, connects to YouTube when you press play.

Your rights

You can request access to and a copy of your data, correction, deletion, restriction of processing and, where the GDPR provides for it, data portability. You can object to processing based on a legitimate interest. You can withdraw consent at any time. Withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of earlier processing. You can also lodge a complaint with the President of the Polish Personal Data Protection Office. Information from Poland’s data protection authority
